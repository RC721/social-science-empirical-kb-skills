# Claim and Relationship Rules

## Claims

Write atomic, bounded claims. A single full-text study may support a `single-study/preliminary` formal claim, but never label it field consensus. Link it to exact Finding and Evidence IDs. A multi-study claim requires independent-study checking, compatible scope and measurement, method comparison, and a search for limiting or conflicting evidence; its promotion is a human gate.

Every claim states population, context, period, constructs, direction or relationship, evidence type, confidence, causal status, and what remains unproven. Observational evidence cannot be rewritten as causal.

## Relationships

Use only `support`, `limit`, `conflict`, `extend`, `replicate`, `version`, `measurement-difference`, `context-difference`. Formal relations require paper evidence or verified identity evidence. Semantic similarity may create a candidate only.

## Concepts

Create a Concept when at least three papers use it materially or when a definitional or measurement dispute justifies one. Include working definition, literature differences, operationalizations, linked claims, disputes, and sources. Do not turn ordinary keywords into Concepts.
